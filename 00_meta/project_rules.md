# Creative Rules — The Bin

> Defines the specific rules for this narrative universe.

## Identity

- **Working title:** The Bin
- **Genre:** Urban animal micro-drama / slice-of-life noir
- **Setting:** Present-day Chinatown, mostly at night, behind and inside the Golden Carp restaurant.
- **Language:** English prose and primarily English dialogue. Brief code-switching is allowed when a character profile explicitly calls for it.

## Core Premise

Behind the restaurant, the alley has its own social order. Humans think the space is for trash, smoke breaks, and deliveries; animals know it as territory, court, refuge, and feeding ground. Conflict is rarely open. Rank is expressed through stillness, timing, distance, and who moves first.

## Writing

- **POV:** Third-person limited. Prefer animal perception when the scene is centered on the cats; use restrained human close perspective only when the scene moves into the kitchen or follows Liang.
- **Tense:** Narrative present unless an existing chapter has established simple past. Keep tense consistent inside each chapter.
- **Tone:** Nocturnal, sensory, restrained, quietly tense, sometimes dryly funny.
- **Scale:** Keep stakes small but emotionally precise: food, warmth, rank, habit, fatigue, mercy.
- **Conflict:** Favor posture, silence, glances, waiting, and tacit agreements over action scenes.
- **Animal interiority:** Do not make animals sound like humans in costumes. Their logic is bodily, sensory, immediate, territorial, and associative.
- **Human dialogue:** Sparse and concrete. Liang may joke, grumble, or tease, but rarely explains his kindness. Liang speaks fluent English with a Chinese accent and occasional words from his own language; keep it clear, restrained, and never caricatured.
- Read `00_meta/style_reference.md` before generating prose.

## Voices and Behavior

- **Daphne:** stillness, precise movement, refusal to waste force.
- **Milo:** sudden motion, curiosity, appetite, mistakes disguised as play.
- **Liang Wei:** fluent English with accented cadence, short amused fragments, occasional Chinese words when irritated or tired; irritation used as cover for care.
- **Mei-Lin Chen:** clipped, managerial, practical.
- **Old Bao:** weight, breath, old patience.
- **Silence:** is part of the social order. Describe what changes inside silence instead of filling it.

## Do Not

- Do not turn animal dynamics into explicit human dialogue unless the user asks for fable mode.
- Do not escalate every contest into a fight.
- Do not make Daphne cruel; her authority is controlled, not theatrical.
- Do not make Milo stupid; he is inexperienced and impulsive.
- Do not make Liang sentimental in direct narration; show his kindness through action.
