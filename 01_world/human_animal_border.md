# Human-Animal Border

## Core Rules

The kitchen door is the central border between human order and animal order. It opens for trash, smoke, deliveries, heat, and mistakes. Crossing it changes what kind of danger a character faces.

## Human Side

- Rules are spoken: orders, complaints, timing, money, hygiene, reputation.
- Mei-Lin represents visible discipline.
- Liang represents practical exception: he knows rules, breaks small ones, and hides mercy as waste management.

## Animal Side

- Rules are enacted: posture, waiting, yielding, access, scars, habit.
- Daphne represents local authority.
- Old Bao represents memory and weight.
- Milo represents pressure from youth and appetite.

## Border Events

- A cat enters the kitchen.
- Liang leaves scraps near the threshold.
- Trash is carried out.
- Mei-Lin notices or nearly notices the feeding.
- The door closes at the wrong moment.

## Limits / Costs

- If animals are seen by the wrong human, feeding routes may close.
- If Liang feeds one animal too obviously, he can disturb the alley hierarchy.
- If Milo grows too bold indoors, kindness may become risk.

## Common Reactions

- Liang pretends his kindness is irritation.
- Daphne treats human offerings as useful but not authoritative.
- Milo overreads access as permission.
- Mei-Lin reads disorder before she reads attachment.

## Narrative Uses

- Create tension without violence.
- Show character through repeated threshold behavior.
- Let the same object have two meanings: trash to humans, wealth to animals.

## Open Questions

- Will Mei-Lin ever directly intervene in the animal order?
- Has Liang fed animals before Daphne and Milo?
