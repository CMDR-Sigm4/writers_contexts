# Night Rhythm

## Core Rules

Night is when the alley becomes legible. The human day narrows into restaurant cleanup, trash runs, smoke breaks, delivery leftovers, and the last customers leaving. The animal world expands as traffic thins and predictable food sources appear.

## Phases

### Dinner Rush

- Kitchen heat peaks.
- Trash accumulates but is not yet taken out.
- Animals wait near vents, under crates, and beneath parked scooters.

### Closing Drift

- Bags are tied.
- Doors open more often.
- Cigarettes appear.
- Scraps become possible.

### After-Midnight Quiet

- The alley cools.
- The best food is gone or claimed.
- Rank matters more because there are fewer distractions.

## Limits / Costs

- Injury at night is dangerous. A cat that cannot move loses food, heat, and hiding places.
- Noise draws humans, dogs, or unwanted animals.
- Too much boldness around the kitchen risks being locked in, kicked out, or noticed by Mei-Lin.

## Common Reactions

- Daphne waits through the rush; she does not waste effort early.
- Milo is most likely to make mistakes during closing drift, when smells intensify and doors open.
- Liang's smoke break marks a temporary softening of the human border.

## Narrative Uses

- Structure scenes around kitchen phases instead of clock time.
- Use repeated sounds as cues: cleaver strikes, trash bag drag, lighter click, vent hum.
- Let small changes in routine feel dramatic to the animals.

## Open Questions

- Does the Golden Carp always close at the same time?
- What happens on rainy nights, festival nights, or inspection nights?
