# Modern Chinatown

## Core Rules

The story takes place in a present-day Chinatown district where public brightness and private labor exist side by side. The street is neon, food windows, delivery scooters, late customers, and reflected rain. The service alleys are older, darker, and more honest about the cost of keeping the storefronts alive.

## Social Texture

- Family restaurants, small groceries, late-night kitchens, delivery workers, tired owners, smokers in back doors.
- Tourists and customers mostly see the public street, not the alley systems behind it.
- Regulars recognize rhythms: which kitchen closes late, which door sticks, where steam leaks, which bins smell richest.

## Limits / Weaknesses

- Do not make the district exoticized or decorative. It is lived-in, working, practical, and modern.
- Avoid treating Chinatown as a single monolith. The story focuses on one restaurant block and its immediate back-of-house ecosystem.
- Keep details concrete: signage, wet asphalt, vents, cardboard, kitchen exhaust, delivery bags, shuttered storefronts.

## Common Reactions

- Humans move through the alley with purpose: trash, smoke, deliveries, shortcuts.
- Animals read the same place differently: heat, scent, cover, food, rank, threat.
- Most people ignore the animals unless they become inconvenient.

## Narrative Uses

- Contrast the bright public restaurant front with the smoky service alley.
- Let city sounds mark time without needing exposition.
- Use weather and closing routines to change animal access to food and shelter.

## Open Questions

- Which city is this Chinatown in?
- Is the Golden Carp an old family place, a struggling business, or simply ordinary and tired?
