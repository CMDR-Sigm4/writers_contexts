# Alley Social Order

## Core Rule

The animals behind the Golden Carp follow a hierarchy based on seniority, territorial memory, controlled force, and practical usefulness. Size matters less than judgment.

## Daphne's Authority

Daphne controls the main trash bin because the other animals accept that she knows when to escalate and when to wait. She does not need constant displays of violence. Stillness is part of her rank.

## Milo's Position

Milo is tolerated because he is young, useful as a warning signal, and not yet a true threat. His mistakes are corrected through posture, distance, and denied access before they become fights.

## Old Bao's Position

Old Bao is physically larger than the cats, but he does not challenge Daphne inside the alley's feeding order. His deference confirms that the hierarchy is older and subtler than strength.

## Human Interface

Liang Wei affects the animal order by distributing scraps, but he does not control it. The animals accept his food without treating him as ruler of the alley.

## Limits

- Open fights are rare because injury is expensive.
- Hissing, striking, and chasing happen only when posture fails.
- Food access can be granted without implying equality.

## Narrative Uses

- Quiet contests over scraps or warm spots.
- New animals misreading Daphne's tolerance.
- Liang accidentally disrupting the order by feeding one animal first.

## Open Questions

- What past event established Daphne's reputation?
- Has Old Bao ever defended the alley animals?
