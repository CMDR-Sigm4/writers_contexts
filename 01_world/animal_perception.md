# Animal Perception

## Core Rules

Animal-centered scenes should privilege sensory and spatial logic over human-style explanation. Cats and dogs understand the alley through smell, temperature, movement, pressure, hunger, and repeated patterns.

## Cats

- Smell carries history: who passed, what food is fresh, whether a human is angry, whether rain has washed a path clean.
- Distance is language. A step forward, a pause, a sideways retreat, or a raised paw can be more meaningful than action.
- Stillness can be force. Daphne uses stillness as a claim.
- Play can disguise uncertainty. Milo turns mistakes into stretches, head tilts, and sudden changes of direction.

## Dogs

- Weight and breath matter. Old Bao communicates by choosing when not to move.
- He reads routine and age more than quick motion.
- His respect for Daphne is not submission to a cat; it is recognition of a local law.

## Limits

- Do not give animals abstract political or philosophical thoughts.
- Do not make animal interiority cute or overly verbal.
- Do not explain every gesture; let readers infer from behavior.

## Common Reactions

- A new smell interrupts attention.
- Heat draws bodies.
- Sudden human sound freezes movement.
- Food creates pressure but not always violence.

## Narrative Uses

- Rewrite human events as sensory events when seen by animals.
- Use body language as dialogue.
- Make silence active: a social decision, not an empty pause.

## Open Questions

- How much animal interiority should future chapters use?
- Should different animals have distinct sensory emphases?
